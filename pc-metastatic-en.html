FOLLOW-UP AND RECURRENCE

After it spreads, the order of treatments

Every step that lengthens survival has a price and a trial population. And in Taiwan one reimbursement rule decides your sequence before any of them.

Prostate Cancer Guide · Follow-up and Recurrence · 25 references
#Prostate#Recurrence#Hormone Therapy#Chemotherapy#NHI Coverage

Metastatic prostate cancer is now measured in years, with an order that can be planned rather than improvised. The conversation that matters most — which agent goes first — has to happen at the first line, not when the first one stops working.

The moment "metastatic" appears on a report, your sense of time collapses. Let me pull the timeline back out: metastatic prostate cancer is now measured in years, with a defined order of treatments that can be planned in advance. This piece is about that order — and behind every step that lengthens survival I put the price and the trial population, in the same paragraph. One Taiwanese rule determines your sequence more than any hazard ratio here. It is in the middle. Do not skip it.

"It is still hormone-sensitive" — that sentence decides how the next year goes

Castration alone is no longer standard. CHAARTED enrolled 790 men with metastatic hormone-sensitive disease (median age 63), median follow-up 28.9 months: adding docetaxel gave median overall survival 57.6 against 44.0 months (hazard ratio 0.61, 0.47–0.80; P<0.001) — a hazard ratio is the ratio of the rate at which events occur in one period, so 0.61 means the rate of death fell to about six-tenths, not that "six out of ten survived". The price is chemotherapy's: grade 3 to 4 febrile neutropenia 6.2%, infection with neutropenia 2.3%[1].

A meta-analysis of three trials, 2,992 men with M1 disease, draws the boundary sharply: adding docetaxel gave a hazard ratio for death of 0.77 (0.68–0.87) and a 9% absolute difference in four-year survival; in the 2,121 men without metastases (M0), no survival benefit (hazard ratio 0.87, 0.69–1.09; p=0.218)[2]. Same drug, different men, different answer — a theme that recurs here.

Four trials of four new hormonal agents, four different bills

LATITUDE, 1,199 men with newly diagnosed high-risk metastatic disease: abiraterone gave median survival not reached against 34.7 months (hazard ratio 0.62), the bill grade 3 hypertension and hypokalaemia[3]. STAMPEDE's abiraterone arm, 1,917 men (52% metastatic): hazard ratio for death 0.63 (0.52–0.76); grade 3 to 5 adverse events 47% against 33%, nine grade 5 (fatal) events on abiraterone against three in controls[4]. TITAN, 1,052 men, 24-month survival 82.4% against 73.5% (hazard ratio 0.67); grade 3 to 4 adverse events 42.2% against 40.8%, more rash on apalutamide[5]. ENZAMET, 1,125 men, three-year survival 80% against 72% (hazard ratio 0.67); 33 men against 14 stopped for adverse events, seizures in 7 men (1%) against none, and the authors point specifically at higher toxicity in men also given early docetaxel[6].

These are medians for the trial populations, not predictions for you. CHAARTED's median age was 63, TITAN's 68; a man of 78 with heart disease and diabetes was not in these trials.

Is the triplet better than the doublet? Nobody has made that comparison

ARASENS, 1,306 men (86.1% already metastatic at diagnosis), added darolutamide on top of castration plus docetaxel: risk of death down 32.5% (hazard ratio 0.68, 0.57–0.80). Grade 3 to 4 adverse events 66.1% against 63.5% — two-thirds in both arms, because both were having chemotherapy, commonest neutropenia (33.7% against 34.2%)[7]. "About the same as the control arm" is not "mild". PEACE-1, 1,172 men with de novo metastatic disease, added abiraterone on top of castration plus docetaxel: in that population overall survival hazard ratio 0.75 (0.59–0.95); grade 3 or higher adverse events 63% against 52%, the largest single difference hypertension (22% against 13%)[8].

One sentence has to go down here: the control arm in both ARASENS and PEACE-1 was "castration plus docetaxel", and no trial has randomised the triplet against a modern doublet of castration plus a new hormonal agent[7][8]. So "the triplet beats the doublet" is a claim no trial supports; I will not say it, and if you hear it you may ask which trial made that comparison.

Should the prostate still be irradiated once it has spread? It depends how many deposits there are

STAMPEDE's prostate radiotherapy arm enrolled 2,061 men, and the overall result was negative: unselected, hazard ratio for overall survival 0.92 (0.80–1.06), p=0.266, three-year survival 62% control against 65% with prostate radiotherapy — the direction favours radiotherapy, but unselected it did not reach statistical significance. The information is in the split: low metastatic burden (819 men, 40%) hazard ratio 0.68 (0.52–0.90), three-year survival 81% against 73%; high metastatic burden (1,120 men) hazard ratio 1.07 (0.90–1.28), three-year survival 53% against 54% — no benefit. The trial used the CHAARTED definition: high burden is four or more bone metastases with at least one outside the vertebral bodies and pelvis, or visceral metastases. During radiotherapy 48 men (5%) had grade 3 to 4 RTOG events[9].

So ask it straight: do my bone scan and CT count as low or high burden? The answer determines whether irradiating the prostate helps you, or only adds side effects.

National Health Insurance funds one new hormonal agent for life, and that rule matters more than any hazard ratio

Sections 9.49 (abiraterone), 9.54 (enzalutamide), 9.90 (apalutamide) and 9.94 (darolutamide) of the National Health Insurance Administration's (健保署) Drug Reimbursement Regulations (全民健康保險藥品給付規定) each carry one identical sentence: 「不論病人處於轉移或非轉移的狀態下，新型荷爾蒙藥品(abiraterone、apalutamide、darolutamide 和 enzalutamide)，及 olaparib 與 abiraterone 併用，終生僅給付一種治療，且除非出現嚴重不耐受反應導致必須永久停止治療，不得互換。」— whether or not the patient's disease is metastatic, among the new hormonal agents (abiraterone, apalutamide, darolutamide and enzalutamide), and olaparib combined with abiraterone, only one treatment is reimbursed in a lifetime, and no switching is permitted unless a severe intolerance forces permanent discontinuation[10][11][12][13]

Plainly: of these four drugs, National Health Insurance (健保) pays for one in your lifetime, and once used you cannot change. International guidelines will not mention it, because they do not deal with differences between national payers — but in Taiwan it determines your sequence more than any survival curve. Which is why the conversation about "which one do we use first" has to happen at the first line, not when the first one stops working.

The same documents carry other rules you meet directly. The National Health Insurance definition of high-risk metastatic castration-sensitive disease requires two of three: Gleason score ≥8; three or more lesions on bone scan with at least one outside the spine and pelvis; visceral metastases[10] — three lesions, not CHAARTED's four, and the two must not be used interchangeably. In the castration-sensitive setting these agents are capped at 36 months in total, require prior review, and must be re-applied for every 3 months[12]. From 1 April 2026 (115 年 4 月 1 日) darolutamide may be used 「作為單一療法或與 docetaxel 併用」 — as monotherapy or with docetaxel — for high-risk metastatic castration-sensitive disease, docetaxel limited to 6 cycles when combined[13]; docetaxel's own reimbursement includes 「與 darolutamide 併用於治療高風險轉移性的去勢敏感性前列腺癌」, use combined with darolutamide for high-risk metastatic castration-sensitive prostate cancer[14].

After castration resistance: ask of every option what it buys and what it costs

In COU-AA-302 abiraterone extended radiographic progression-free survival from 8.3 to 16.5 months, hazard ratio for overall survival 0.75, but that analysis did not cross the prespecified efficacy boundary; toxicity concentrated in mineralocorticoid effects and liver-function abnormalities[15]. In PREVAIL enzalutamide gave a hazard ratio for death of 0.71 (0.60–0.84), the common clinically relevant adverse events fatigue and hypertension[16]. In Taiwan this slot has usually already been spent by the lifetime one-agent rule.

On chemotherapy, TROPIC enrolled 755 men progressing after docetaxel: cabazitaxel gave median survival 15.1 against 12.7 months (hazard ratio 0.70), and the price is real: grade 3 or higher neutropenia 82%, febrile neutropenia 8%, grade 3 or higher diarrhoea 6%[17]. CARD enrolled 255 men who had had docetaxel and one new hormonal agent: cabazitaxel gave overall survival 13.6 against 11.0 months (hazard ratio 0.64), grade 3 or higher adverse events 56.3% against 52.4% — the comparator was no easy ride either[18]. Section 9.137 requires at least 3 prior cycles of docetaxel, progression after a new hormonal agent, and performance status ECOG ≤1; the course is capped at 7 cycles and may not be combined with a new hormonal agent or olaparib[19].

Radium-223 (ALSYMPCA) enrolled 921 castration-resistant men with symptomatic bone metastases and no known visceral metastases: median survival 14.9 against 11.3 months (hazard ratio 0.70), low rates of marrow suppression[20]. Section 9.68 is written to that same population: symptomatic bone metastases, at least two lesions on imaging, no visceral metastases, six cycles maximum, prior review — and 「不得合併使用 abiraterone、enzalutamide 及其他治療因惡性腫瘤伴隨骨骼事件之藥品，如 denosumab、bisphosphonates 等」, it may not be combined with abiraterone, enzalutamide, or other drugs for cancer-related skeletal events such as denosumab and bisphosphonates[21]. That is why the bone injections stop while you are on radium-223.

¹⁷⁷Lu-PSMA (VISION) is often called "targeted, so it is gentle"; the numbers do not say that. 831 men were randomised of 1,179 screened — three in ten excluded because their PSMA PET did not qualify — median survival 15.3 against 11.3 months (hazard ratio 0.62), and grade 3 or higher adverse events 52.7% against 38.0%[22]. I found no National Health Insurance reimbursement document for it in the drug rules database, so ask your case manager (個管師) or the hospital billing office (醫務課).

PARP inhibitors need a genetic condition. PROfound, 387 men with homologous recombination repair gene alterations progressing after a new hormonal agent: in the BRCA1/BRCA2/ATM group (245 men) median survival 19.1 against 14.7 months (hazard ratio 0.69), though 66% of controls later crossed to olaparib[23]. Section 9.85 restricts it to pathogenic or likely pathogenic BRCA1/2 variants with progression after a new hormonal agent, with prior review, re-application every 3 months, a BRCA report at first application, and no combination with chemotherapy[24].

Genetic testing: this one does not only concern you

In 692 men with metastatic prostate cancer tested without selection by family history or age, 82 (11.8%) carried a presumed deleterious germline DNA-repair gene mutation: BRCA2 5.3%, ATM 1.6%, BRCA1 0.9%. In the same study men with localised disease ran 4.6% and 53,105 people without a cancer diagnosis 2.7%; and the proportion carrying a mutation did not differ by family history of prostate cancer or by age at diagnosis[25]. That is why "nobody in my family has had it" is no reason to skip testing.

Two kinds of test must be kept apart. Germline testing looks at what you were born with and pass on, so it bears on screening advice for your siblings and children and should be done where genetic counselling is available; somatic testing of tumour tissue looks at the tumour itself, and decides whether a PARP inhibitor applies. What next-generation sequencing covers in Taiwan, and for which cancers, changes over time — ask your case manager (個管師) or the hospital billing office (醫務課).

What has to be phoned in the same day

Two things cannot wait for the next clinic. First, fever during chemotherapy. Grade 3 or higher neutropenia reached 82% in TROPIC[17], and fever during the days when the counts are low is an emergency: go straight to the emergency department and say you are on chemotherapy — do not take a paracetamol at home and watch. Second: new back pain, together with leg weakness, numbness, or a change in bladder or bowel function — this is spinal cord compression, go to hospital the same day, do not wait. "Back pain plus a weak leg: do not wait" gives that a full section, including why waiting until tomorrow can mean the function does not come back.

Take three questions into your next appointment: am I low or high burden? Which new hormonal agent goes first, and why that one — given National Health Insurance pays for one in my lifetime? And am I a candidate for germline and tumour testing, and what would the result change? Settle the order early and every step after is easier to walk.

From the physician

Trial medians are not personal predictions, and low versus high metastatic burden changes what helps you. Ask which burden you are, which new hormonal agent is being chosen first and why, and whether germline and tumour testing apply to you.
