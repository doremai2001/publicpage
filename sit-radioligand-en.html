BEYOND STANDARD TREATMENT

One injection, and the radiation finds its own way

The ligand decides where the drug goes, the isotope what it emits. Three drugs, five phase 3 trials, and the answers do not agree with each other.

Situation Guides · Beyond Standard Treatment · 10 references
#Newer therapies#Radiotherapy#Reading evidence#Regulation#Decisions

Most people ask about the effect and few ask about the scan — but the scan is the first gate. If the PET cannot find the target, the drug cannot be delivered, and that is not "we could still try", it is a closed road. So: can it be done, how long, and how far the data goes.

"One injection and it reaches every metastasis." I have heard that in clinic many times, and half of it is true. It is radiation; it just does not come out of a machine, it is carried to the cancer cell by the bloodstream. The half that is not true is "one": this is a course of a fixed number of treatments over several months, and before it starts you have to prove the target is on your tumour.

Two halves to the drug: the ligand decides where, the isotope decides what

These drugs are two components joined together. The ligand recognises something on the surface of the cancer cell and decides where the radiation is delivered; the isotope emits the radiation and decides what kind, how far it travels and for how long. Three are in clinical use:

  • Lu-177 PSMA-617: the ligand recognises prostate-specific membrane antigen; lutetium-177 is a beta emitter with a range of a few millimetres, and it also gives off some gamma, so after the injection you can image where it has gone.

  • Ra-223 (radium-223 dichloride): this one has no ligand. Radium is a chemical relative of calcium and goes by itself to where bone turnover is high, which is where osteoblastic bone metastases are; radium-223 emits alpha radiation, with a range of a few cell widths, which is why its marrow toxicity is low.

  • Lu-177 DOTATATE: the ligand is a somatostatin analogue, recognising somatostatin receptors on well-differentiated neuroendocrine tumours.

If the scan is negative, this treatment has nothing to do

This is the precondition patients most often underestimate. VISION required a ⁶⁸Ga-PSMA-11 PET confirming PSMA positivity before randomisation[1]; NETTER-2 required every target lesion to be somatostatin receptor positive[2]. If the ligand cannot find a handle, the drug does not get delivered. A negative scan does not mean "we could still give it a try". It means this road is closed.

The schedule is fixed, and it goes on top of standard treatment

The rhythm of all three is set out in the trials: Lu-177 PSMA every 6 weeks, 4 to 6 doses, roughly 6 to 9 months[1]; Ra-223 every 4 weeks, 6 injections, about half a year[3]; Lu-177 DOTATATE every 8 weeks, 4 doses, with long-acting somatostatin injections alongside, about 8 months[2]. Each is a short intravenous injection or infusion; with Lu-177 DOTATATE an amino acid infusion runs alongside to protect the kidneys. Whether you stay overnight depends on local radiation protection rules, not on how ill you are.

Before each dose, blood counts and renal function are checked; a dose is delayed or stopped if they are inadequate. In VISION it was given on top of standard care, and the control arm received the same standard care[1] — not one drug in this class is intended to replace the treatment you are already on.

Three drugs, five phase 3 trials, and the answers do not agree

  • Lu-177 PSMA, after chemotherapy. VISION was an international, open-label phase 3 trial, 831 patients randomised 2:1; radiographic progression-free survival 8.7 against 3.4 months, overall survival 15.3 against 11.3 months (hazard ratio 0.62 — a hazard ratio compares how fast events accrue in the two arms, not a multiple of probability; 0.62 in plain words means deaths arrived more slowly in the treated arm of this dataset, not "38% fewer people die"); grade 3 or worse adverse events 52.7% against 38.0%[1]. That result applies only to PSMA-PET-positive castration-resistant prostate cancer after one androgen receptor pathway inhibitor and one or two lines of taxane.

  • Move the same drug one line earlier and the answer changes. PSMAfore enrolled patients who had not had chemotherapy and would otherwise have switched to another hormonal agent, 234 against 234; radiographic progression was indeed delayed, but final overall survival was 24.48 against 23.13 months, hazard ratio 0.91, p=0.20, no statistical difference. The investigators note that 141 patients in the control arm (60.3%) later crossed over to the same drug[4]. Using the VISION result to argue for earlier use is the commonest line crossed on this topic. One line earlier and the answer changed: that is exactly what sits between new and better — "New does not mean better".

  • Ra-223. ALSYMPCA is the rare double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial in this piece, 921 patients: overall survival 14.9 against 11.3 months, hazard ratio 0.70, low myelosuppression, fewer adverse events than in the placebo arm, and the trial stopped early at interim analysis for efficacy[3]. It treats bone metastases only — radium follows the calcium road, it does not reach visceral metastases, and it is not a substitute for treating them.

  • Lu-177 DOTATATE in progressive midgut neuroendocrine tumours. NETTER-1 was positive on progression-free survival, but the pre-specified final overall survival analysis did not reach statistical significance (48.0 against 36.3 months, p=0.30); on long-term follow-up 2 of 111 patients (1.8%) developed myelodysplastic syndrome[5].

  • Lu-177 DOTATATE first line, in higher-grade disease. NETTER-2 enrolled 226 patients: progression-free survival 22.8 against 8.5 months, hazard ratio 0.276[2]. That is a large gap, but read the endpoint out loud: progression-free survival, not overall survival — and the same drug's overall survival result in NETTER-1 did not reach significance.

Back at home: there is no general answer to "how many days"

What the rules limit is not you; it is the people around you. Article 12 of the Ionising Radiation Protection Safety Standards (游離輻射防護安全標準) sets an effective dose limit of 1 millisievert a year for a member of the public, and Article 6 states that an individual's dose excludes the person's own medical exposure[6] (verified 2026-08-30).

For a sense of magnitude: measurements in 47 patients given ¹⁷⁷Lu-PSMA found a dose rate at one metre of 31.6 ± 8 µSv/h in the first hour, falling below 30 µSv/h within 2 to 3 hours and below 20 after 6 hours; measured effective doses to carers came out between 20 and 1,170 µSv, all under international limits[7]. But that is a dosimetry observation in 47 people, not a rule, and I am not going to convert it into a number of days.

The number of days is calculated per patient in any case. A 20-patient ¹⁷⁷Lu-DOTATATE study showed whole-body clearance to be biexponential: using only the fast phase measurable before discharge systematically underestimates it, so the authors derived a correction of a further 46 hours before setting the restriction period[8]. So the right move is this: before discharge, get a written home radiation-protection sheet that states, item by item, how many days each restriction lasts — distance from other people and time spent with them (children and pregnant women especially), sitting to urinate, flushing twice, washing clothes separately. Those days are calculated from your residual activity on the day you leave, not copied from a general figure on the internet.

One of the three is reimbursed in Taiwan; two are not

Ra-223 is reimbursed, on tight conditions. Drug reimbursement rule 9.68 (effective 2019-03-01) limits it to castration-resistant prostate cancer with symptomatic bone metastases and no visceral metastases; two or more bone lesions; a maximum of six courses per patient; prior review is required, with imaging from within three months showing two or more bone metastases and no visceral metastasis, plus records of regular analgesic use; and it may not be combined with abiraterone, enzalutamide, denosumab or bisphosphonates and other bone-event drugs[9] (115.08.21 version, verified 2026-08-30). There is also a procedure code, 26078A "radium-223 treatment procedure fee", 2,143 points (effective 2023-10-01), whose payment note specifies one dose every four weeks for six courses, operators being a nuclear medicine specialist with nuclear-medicine-trained radiographers and nurses, premises holding a radioactive materials licence — and this item does not include the radium-223 injection itself[10].

Lu-177 PSMA and Lu-177 DOTATATE are not reimbursed. I searched the full fee schedule and Section 9 of the drug reimbursement rules for "Lutetium", "鑥", "PSMA", "DOTATATE" and "radiopharmaceutical", and found nothing[10]. Whether these two hold a Taiwanese drug licence, and where they can be obtained, I found no citable source for; I am writing neither that they do nor that they do not — ask nuclear medicine directly. There is no official price list for the self-pay version either; how to get a written itemisation is in "How to ask about a self-pay item". The overall sequencing after prostate cancer spreads is in "After it spreads, the order of treatments". Holding a licence, being reimbursed, and having evidence are three separate enquiries — "Approved, covered, effective: three different things".

The first gate is the scan, not your willingness

  1. Is my scan positive? Which tracer was it done with? This is the first gate on whether it can be done at all, not a formality.

  2. How many doses are planned? What is checked before each one? What would delay or stop it? Kidneys and marrow are the dose-limiting organs for the lutetium drugs.

  3. What are my home restrictions, and how many days does each last? Please give me it in writing. While you are there, find out who to phone if something happens between doses.

From the physician

Get the home radiation-protection sheet in writing before discharge: distance from other people and time spent with them, sitting to urinate, washing clothes separately — how many days each lasts is calculated from your own residual activity on the day, not copied from the internet. And one line to keep: all three drugs go on top of standard care, and VISION's control arm received the same standard care.
