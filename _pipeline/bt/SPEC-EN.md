## 2. The editorial stance, restated for English

This topic's danger runs **opposite to the glioblastoma topic's**. There the enemy is despair;
here it is **over-treatment, over-panic, and the two ways "benign" gets misread**. Both directions
have to be blocked, often inside the same paragraph:

- "benign" → "so I can ignore it" — forbidden;
- "brain tumour" → "brain cancer" → "so operate on it now" — equally forbidden.

English makes the first failure easier, because "benign" is a stronger reassurance in English than
「良性」 is in Chinese. **Never let "benign" sit at the end of a paragraph as the last word.**
Wherever the Chinese qualifies it, the English qualifies it at the same strength or harder.

**Every single number carries its "which tumour" label** — tumour type, WHO grade, size stratum,
functioning or not, study design, follow-up length, n. A figure that reads as applying to all three
diseases is a failure even when the arithmetic is right.

## 3. The passages English will break

- **紅線 1 / 修正 2 / 修正 3 (`bt-incidental`)** — the topic's highest risk, bidirectional.
  「大多數偶然發現的腦膜瘤沒有長大」 **does not exist in either language.** The English is
  "most of them do not end up causing trouble", with the ascertainment method named in the same
  sentence: Behbahani 2019 (n=64, >15% volume change) **75% grew**; the IMPACT external validation
  (JAMA Oncol 2026, 33 centres, 15 countries, 1,248 people / 1,010 untreated meningiomas, median
  follow-up 61 months) **10-year progression-free 85.7%**. The two do not conflict; the definition
  of "grew" does. Millward 2024 (33 studies, 268 verbatim outcomes, only 77 defined) is the
  field-level backing and stays.
  The incidence denominators are **three, not one** — Bos 2016 (Rotterdam, n=5,800, mean 64.9
  years, brain MRI) meningioma 2.5%; Morris 2009 (BMJ meta-analysis, 19,559 people) neoplastic
  incidental findings 0.70% (0.47–0.98), **high-resolution 4.3% vs standard 1.7%**; Hall 1994
  (100 normal volunteers, dedicated high-resolution contrast pituitary MRI) **10% with 3–6 mm
  lesions read as adenoma**; EANO 2021's own wording is "0.9% to 1.0%". All of them keep their
  scan-and-population label. **No sentence may let the reader decide for themselves whether to be
  treated** — what the article gives is a list of questions to take to the clinic.
- **紅線 3 / 修正 12 (`bt-pit-prolactinoma`)**, the second most dangerous article.
  "Pituitary tumours are treated with drugs first" must be **impossible to construct**: only
  prolactinoma is drug-first. **Every sentence that says "drugs first" contains the word
  "prolactinoma", and the same paragraph states that first-line for somatotroph adenoma and
  Cushing's disease is surgery.** The 2025 prolactinoma consensus's recommendation 10/21 is
  quoted at full strength — for **Knosp 0–1, well-circumscribed micro- and macroadenomas, surgery
  and medical therapy are both first-line** — while the direction is unchanged for Knosp ≥2 and
  giant tumours ("DA is the first choice"). Stopping the drug is written **only** as "what the
  doctor is weighing up", with the outcomes after withdrawal in the same paragraph (**21% is the pooled
  proportion whose prolactin STAYED NORMAL after withdrawal — it is not a recurrence rate**; the
  other two datasets report recurrence of 54% and 63%, each labelled with its cohort). **No dose, in any unit, enters a patient-facing sentence, and nothing
  may read as "you can stop the tablets yourself".**
- **紅線 4 / 修正 8 (`bt-mg-hormone`)**. "The pill causes meningioma" must be unconstructible:
  the overall no-association finding for combined oral contraceptives comes **first**, then the
  specific progestogens with their dose–exposure windows. **Absolute and relative risk share one
  frame** (cyproterone acetate 23.8 vs 4.5 per 100,000 person-years). Taiwan's shape has changed:
  **nomegestrol (Zoely) and chlormadinone marketing authorisations have been cancelled, as has
  injectable MPA; only cyproterone remains** — and the Taiwan FDA's 111-year order requiring the
  contraindication section to carry 「現罹患腦膜瘤或有腦膜瘤病史之病人」 is translated as a
  translation of the order, marked as one. **Nothing may read as an instruction to stop the drug**
  — stopping is the prescriber's decision, and the article's job is to say whom to ask. No
  specialty and no prescribing habit is named or criticised.
- **紅線 5 / 修正 9 / 修正 10 / 修正 11 (`bt-an-choice`, `bt-an-hearing`)**.
  The one honest sentence, in its corrected form: **"there is one randomised trial here, and what
  it compared was radiosurgery against watching, with tumour volume ratio as the primary endpoint;
  how surgery compares with radiosurgery is still only retrospective cohorts and meta-analyses."**
  V-REX (JAMA 2023, n=100, 4 years, single centre, observer-blinded) keeps volume ratio 0.87 vs
  1.51, ratio 1.73, p=.002, **25 of 26 prespecified secondary endpoints not significantly
  different**, 42% of the observation arm irradiated within 4 years for growth, 56% never treated.
  Direction is blocked **both ways**: not "radiosurgery preserves hearing better", and not
  "watching preserves hearing better" — hearing falls on both paths (Breivik 2013: 76% vs 64%,
  no statistical difference; Rasmussen 2012: none of the irradiated retained it at 10 years) —
  **what radiosurgery buys is tumour control and one fewer re-treatment.**
  **Every hearing-preservation rate carries four labels — which scoring system, how many years,
  n, and which treatment. A rate missing any one of them does not go into the body.**
  Kirchmann 2017 is the load-bearing fact and must survive translation intact: the same 156
  people, the same 9.5 years, **AAO-HNS 34% vs word recognition 58%**. Rahne 2021 (serviceable
  hearing counts drifting between 54 and 60 of the same patients) stays with it.
  "Tumour control" has no agreed definition in the radiosurgery literature (Balossier 2023 lists
  seven or eight, settles on "did not receive further treatment", and says so itself) — so
  **"96% control after radiosurgery" is never set beside "<2% recurrence after gross total
  resection"**; they are two different rulers, and the English says that out loud.
  Size thresholds are quoted as the guidelines word them, differences in wording left side by side.
- **紅線 6 / 修正 14 (`bt-pit-apoplexy`)**. "Seek medical attention if you feel unwell" is a
  failure. The apoplexy symptoms are concrete — **sudden severe headache, rapid change in vision,
  double vision, altered consciousness**. Adrenal insufficiency is written as **four questions to
  get answered in advance**, and **no multiple, no milligram and no number of days may appear**.
  Pallor is a pituitary-origin feature and may be named. The UK guideline cited is the **2016**
  one (Endocrine Connections, open access); the 2011 Clin Endocrinol version is paywalled and is
  not cited. **No instruction to adjust steroid doses, in any form.**
- **紅線 7 / 修正 18 (`bt-radiation-induced`)**, bidirectional. A reader treated with cranial
  radiotherapy in childhood must finish knowing what to do; a reader currently considering
  radiosurgery must **not** finish thinking "irradiation grows a second tumour, so don't".
  CNS 2026 level III is quoted directly — adults with sporadic vestibular schwannoma undergoing
  SRS **should be counselled that SRS does not increase the number of second malignancies**
  relative to the expected rate in the general population. Hasegawa 2025's denominators stay whole:
  1,061 gamma-knife-treated vestibular schwannomas, median follow-up 12 years, **2 malignant
  transformations (0.19%)**, 12,940 lesion-years, annual risk 0.016%, none in the first 5 years;
  the SEER baseline (roughly 1 eighth-nerve MPNST per 1,041 acoustic neuromas in people who had no
  radiation) may sit beside it. **Absolute risk and treatment benefit stay in one paragraph.**
  (Bibliography for these: copy `brief/C.md` `[C-S27]` verbatim — FIXES 丙4.)
- **紅線 8 / 修正 7 (`bt-mg-adjuvant`)**. The two randomised trials are **in different states and
  are never listed together as "ongoing"**: **ROAM/EORTC-1308 closed recruitment on 2021-05-26**
  (target 190, actual 157), results not yet published, Simpson 1–3 eligible; **NRG-BN003 is still
  recruiting**, primary completion 2027-06, eligible after **gross total resection**. The English
  sentence is "one has finished recruiting and is waiting for results, the other is still
  recruiting — and they are not recruiting the same people." RTOG 0539's 10-year data (JCO 2026:
  10-year PFS 85.2% / 72.2% / 42.5% low / intermediate / high risk) is labelled **phase II,
  risk-stratified, non-randomised**; the observational meta-analysis (1,452 people) **finding no
  benefit after gross total resection** sits beside it. Observational and randomised stay
  separately labelled; the Simpson-grade/recurrence association is observational.
- **紅線 9 / 修正 4 / 修正 5 (`bt-three-kinds`)**, the foundation article.
  Prognosis, treatment threshold and follow-up schedule are **never written as one shared set**.
  Other benign intracranial tumours get one signposting sentence each and **nothing that implies
  "they are all much the same".**
  PitNET is **dual nomenclature, not a rename**: WHO 5th edition renamed well-differentiated
  adenohypophyseal tumours PitNET, but publishes as "pituitary adenoma/pituitary neuroendocrine
  tumor (NET)". The objection is quoted at its own strength — overall prevalence above 10%, local
  invasion 1 in 2,000, malignancy 1 in 100,000, therefore the NET label "grossly mislabelling the
  overwhelming majority", with the randomised evidence that disease labels change treatment
  decisions and anxiety. **Both sides stand; "it is called PitNET now" alone is a failure.**
  Taiwan has **no official count of benign brain tumours, and the statutory reason is citable**:
  the Cancer Control Act defines cancer as malignant neoplasm, and the cancer-registry long-form
  manual requires reporting of ICD-O-3 behaviour codes 2, 3, 6 and 9 — **benign (0) and borderline
  (1) are not reported**. The US CBTRUS does the opposite, which is why **every incidence figure in
  this topic comes from a US registry and Taiwan has no counterpart — and that fact is written into
  the article.** D33 (benign neoplasm of brain and other parts of CNS) is the only separable
  Taiwanese figure (113 fiscal year health statistics yearbook: 23,027 outpatient people, 652
  discharges) but **excludes meningioma (D32) and pituitary tumours (D35.2)** and **may not be used
  to stand for either**. The vestibular schwannoma incidence of 2.66 per 100,000 is labelled an
  academic NHI-database study, not official statistics.
- **紅線 10 / 修正 15 (`bt-seizure-steroid`)**. The current guidance is a **level A recommendation
  against**, not "no evidence for routine use": SNO/EANO 2021 — patients with newly diagnosed brain
  tumours who have **not** had a seizure **should not** be started on anti-seizure medication
  (**level A**). Using tumour location, histology, grade, molecular or imaging features to decide
  is **level U (insufficient evidence)** → **nothing may read as "meningiomas are an exception
  because of where they sit or what grade they are".** The perioperative paragraph is level C.
  AAN's 2000 practice parameter was **retired on 2012-06-04** and is not cited.
  The counter-intuitive fact that must be present: peritumoural oedema after meningioma surgery
  mostly does not go away — of 279 people with preoperative oedema taken to gross total resection,
  **96.8% still had oedema change on MRI at least a year later**, mostly gliosis; those in the
  highest tertile of residual oedema volume had **4.7 times the odds** of new-onset seizures
  compared with the lowest. **Nothing may read as "the swelling settles after surgery so the
  tablets can stop".** Driving and epilepsy law follows the version already verified for
  `gb-seizure` (Road Traffic Safety Rules, 115.06.26) and the English wording there;
  if a clause cannot be found, the English says so and points at the motor vehicles office.
- **修正 6 (Taiwan, every article that touches it)**. The catastrophic illness certificate:
  all five sub-items under 「需積極或長期治療之癌症」 are **C codes**, and full-text search of the
  whole schedule returns **zero hits for D32, D33 and D35**. The only item that comes close is
  "complications arising from spinal cord injury or disease" with a **moderate-or-above disability
  threshold**. The English is: "the only line in the schedule that comes anywhere near a benign
  brain tumour is the spinal-cord one, and it has a disability threshold; whether it applies is for
  the insurer to determine — ask the medical affairs office." **Neither "benign brain tumours never
  qualify" nor any hint that they do.** Whether NF2 is on the announced rare-disease list is a
  **gap** and is not written.
- **修正 1 / 修正 20 (NHI stereotactic radiosurgery, `bt-mg-rt`, `bt-an-choice`,
  `bt-pit-surgery-rt`)**. The **115.04.01 (20260401) current schedule governs**; the 20230301
  version that three briefs retrieved is void. **37028B** three-dimensional X-knife stereotactic
  radiosurgery, 82,000 points, effective 20260401, **no "claim by analogy" clause at all**;
  **37029B** image-guided intensity-modulated X-ray stereotactic radiosurgery, 153,229 points,
  effective 20260401, note 3: 「加馬機立體定位放射手術（Stereotactic radiosurgery with γ-knife）
  項目比照申報。」 — translated as a translation. **"CyberKnife" and 「電腦刀」 return zero hits in
  the current schedule and may not be cited**; there is **no separate fractionated-stereotactic
  item**, so **no inference about how the NHI distinguishes single-session from fractionated may be
  drawn from the codes.** Indications name vestibular schwannoma, meningioma, pituitary tumour and
  craniopharyngioma but require one of conditions A–F **and prior case-by-case application for
  every case**; quote the conditions as the clause words them. §一.5① still holds: **no platform,
  no brand, no gamma-knife-versus-linac comparison anywhere in the English.**
- **修正 13 (`bt-pit-surgery-rt`)**. Endoscopic versus microscopic **has randomised trials that
  cannot carry a conclusion**: n=33 and n=36 (the latter **terminated early for slow accrual**);
  the substantive evidence is a 56-study / 26,295-patient meta-analysis. **"Randomised evidence
  supports endoscopic" may not be written.** Pituitary radiotherapy carries three things: tumour
  control; **hormonal remission lagging tumour control by a mean 38 months** (the source says "mean", not median); and the cumulative
  incidence of hypopituitarism after radiotherapy (**5 years 22.4% / 10 years 31.3% / 15 years
  45%**). Protons are never written as superior to photons, and **single-session versus fractionated
  belongs to `bt-mg-rt` — this article does not compare them.**
- **修正 16 (`bt-followup`)**. "When do I stop" is **three different answers, one of which is
  officially 'we don't know'**: meningioma — NICE NG99 has explicit stopping language, "consider
  discharge" after year 9, and for asymptomatic incidental findings a 12-month scan then
  "consider discharge or rescan at 5 years"; non-functioning pituitary adenoma — CNS 2016 states
  **there is insufficient evidence to make a recommendation on the duration and frequency of
  surveillance**, while a 148-patient gross-total-resection cohort concludes lifelong periodic
  imaging is needed; vestibular schwannoma — CNS 2026 goes as far as annual for the first 3 years
  in observed patients and at least every 3 to 5 years thereafter, **with no stopping point**.
- **修正 17 (`bt-recurrence`)**. "There is no drug" is wrong; **"there is no standard drug" is
  right.** Every systemic signal is single-arm PFS-6: sunitinib 42%; bevacizumab (grade 1 87% /
  grade 2 77% / grade 3 46%); everolimus + octreotide 55%; the FAK inhibitor in Alliance A071401
  (grade 1 83%, grade 2/3 33%). **The counter-direction is stronger and is mandatory**: the only
  two randomised datasets are both negative — mifepristone's randomised double-blind phase III
  SWOG S9005 (n=164) negative; trabectedin's randomised phase II EORTC-1320 (n=90) not only failed
  to improve but had **worse PFS than standard care (2.43 vs 4.17 months)** with more toxicity.
  Octreotide alone (n=9) zero responses; pembrolizumab (n=18) PFS-6 11.1%, stopped early for
  futility. Taiwan: full-text search of NHI bevacizumab clause 9.37 returns **zero hits for
  meningioma, schwannoma and NF2** → "the clause does not list this indication; ask the medical
  affairs office how an application would be made", **never an inference either way.** BNCT for
  high-grade recurrent meningioma is **one signposting sentence to "Meningioma: the big trial that
  is never coming"**, not rewritten.
- **修正 19 (`bt-nf2`)**. This is not a rename, it is a **retirement**: quote the 2022 international
  consensus — the term "neurofibromatosis 2" **has been retired** to improve diagnostic
  specificity; the name is **NF2-related schwannomatosis (NF2-SWN)**, and **mosaicism is written
  into the diagnostic criteria** (VAF clearly <50%). **"Multiple meningiomas means NF2" is wrong** —
  LZTR1- and SMARCB1-related schwannomatosis, BAP1, SUFU, SMARCE1 (familial clear-cell meningioma)
  and **previous radiation exposure** all belong in the same differential. "Up to 80% of people
  with NF2-SWN develop meningioma over a lifetime" and bevacizumab hearing response 36–41% (with
  the response definition attached) may be written. **No percentage for a relative's recurrence
  risk, in any form.**
- **Reimbursement register, everywhere**: 「查不到列項，問醫務課／個管師」 → "I could not find a
  listed item — ask the medical affairs office or your case manager". **Never "not covered" as a
  flat claim, and never "so you will have to pay for it yourself".** NHI clause quotations are
  translated faithfully and marked as translations of the clause. Zero-hit full-text searches are
  reported as zero hits. **Media-reported prices are never cited**; self-pay amounts only where an
  official announcement exists, with its date.
- **Institutions**: study sources and official price pages keep their institution names exactly
  where the Chinese has them; none is added; no evaluative adjective attaches to one. **No
  in-house configuration**: no machine, no brand, no "our hospital has / does not have", no naming
  of hospitals. Availability is always "not every hospital has this — ask your own doctor where to
  be referred".
- **Statistics**: an odds ratio is not a multiple of probability — say so where the Chinese says
  so. Non-inferiority proves "not much worse", not "as good as". Correlation is not causation.
  Guideline wording differences are set side by side in the guidelines' own words and **left
  unreconciled**.
- **Word-proportion rule**: 「約三分之二」= "about two-thirds", 「一成」= "10%", 「九成」=
  "nine in ten", 「超過六成」= "more than six in ten" — every number identical, every hedge at
  equal strength.

## 6. Report, don't fix

silently correct it in the English.** Write it into `EN-REPORT.md` with the file, the sentence and
what is wrong. The editor decides, and fixes both languages together. The English edition is this
topic's **third** adversarial review pass, and on the last four topics it was the pass that found
