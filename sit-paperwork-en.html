Beyond the Catastrophic Illness Certificate: what else you can claim

It waives the co-payment, not self-pay; cancer must be applied for, three years or five. The effective date runs from the day you file, not before.

Situation Guides · The First Month · 12 references
#Law & rights#Cost & benefits#The system#Getting Ready

Filling the forms is not the hard part. The hard part is "why did last month's visits not come back", and the answer sits in two dates: when the medical certificate was issued, and the day you filed. Here the statuses are lined up by window, with a version date on every rule.

The hard part about the stack of forms after a diagnosis is not filling them in. It is the order. They are not applied for on the same day, at the same window, or effective from the same day; the wrong order costs a wasted trip, the wrong timing a stretch of what you were entitled to. This piece covers only how each status is obtained, who does it, from which day, what it waives once granted, and what it does not.

The money is not rewritten here: the opportunity cost of leave, hired carers, transport and accommodation are in "The cost that isn't on the receipt"; self-pay items in "How to ask about a self-pay item"; private insurance in "When will insurance pay, and who decides?". Every provision below was verified on 30 August 2026, with its version date — this kind of rule changes faster than you would think.

What the Catastrophic Illness Certificate waives is the co-payment, not self-pay

The legal basis is Article 48 of the National Health Insurance Act (全民健康保險法): an insured person in a situation such as catastrophic illness is exempt from the charges payable under Articles 43 and 47[1]. Article 43 is the outpatient and emergency co-payment, Article 47 the inpatient co-payment. What it waives is the co-payment (部分負擔), not self-pay items.

The NHIA states the scope's edge clearly: what is waived is the illness on the certificate, or treatment the treating physician judges related to it; other treatment at the same visit by the same physician on account of the catastrophic illness; and, in an admission for the catastrophic illness, another specialty's treatment that must run alongside, or catastrophic-illness care the clinical situation requires during the stay[2]. So the other department you saw the same day still charges its co-payment.

Cancer has to be applied for, and the certificate runs for either three years or five

Annex 1 to Article 2 of the Regulations on Exemption from Co-payments for the Insured under National Health Insurance (全民健康保險保險對象免自行負擔費用辦法) (revised 113-09-16, applicable from 114-01-01) has 30 categories in all; the first is "cancers requiring active or long-term treatment", splitting into five sub-paragraphs[3]:

  • Malignant tumour of the thyroid — certificate valid for three years

  • Malignant tumour of the oral cavity, oropharynx or hypopharynx, stage I — three years

  • Malignant tumour of the breast, stage I — three years

  • Malignant tumour of the cervix, stage I — three years

  • Malignant tumours other than the above — five years

Some Annex 1 items are marked as certified by the physician directly, no application needed. Cancer is not among them — an application has to be made[3]. How "stage I" is determined, and how many years your certificate is granted for, follow the NHIA's decision notice, so confirm with your case manager (個管師) or the NHIA regional division (健保署分區業務組).

That medical certificate lasts 30 days, and the effective date does not run backwards

The applicant may be the patient, or an agent the patient appoints, including a hospital or clinic. What to prepare: the application form, a medical certificate of diagnosis (診斷證明書) issued within the past thirty days by a contracted hospital or clinic (the diagnosis field must carry an ICD code), photocopies of identity documents, and material such as a medical record summary or examination reports. It may be posted, delivered in person, or filed by the institution with the regional division through the NHI information service network[4]. The thirty days run from the date the certificate was issued; past that, it has to be issued again.

Three more time points sit in the same regulations: the insurer shall decide and notify within fourteen days, not counting public holidays, of receiving the documents, and the certificate is recorded on the NHI card; the date of application is the effective date; and a fresh application made and approved before expiry keeps the periods continuous, while a late one takes the new application date as its effective date[5]. The effective date not being retroactive is the point most often missed: if the paperwork sits, the visits before it are paid for exactly as they stood. Fourteen days is the statutory processing deadline, not a guarantee of being notified that soon. This affects only how much co-payment you pay, not treatment. Do not postpone a clinic visit, an investigation or treatment while waiting for approval — carry on with what is due, and keep the receipts and payment slips, because what you pay yourself before approval still has the six-month refund route in the next section.

If you have already paid, there is a six-month route

Co-payments already borne before approval may be claimed back as self-advanced medical expenses within six months of the outpatient visit or of discharge, from the NHIA regional division covering the institution that treated you; the applicant may be the insured person, a statutory agent, a statutory heir or an authorised representative[6]. Cases approved during an admission, or where an inpatient test report confirms the diagnosis only after discharge, are provided for separately[2]. Keep the receipts and payment slips.

Disability certification runs on a different track, and you are the one who starts it

The box most often mistaken for "the hospital will do it for me". Article 6, paragraph 1 of the People with Disabilities Rights Protection Act (身心障礙者權益保障法) (amended 114-07-15, promulgated 114-08-01) provides that when the municipal, county or city competent authority accepts an application for assessment from a person with a disability, it shall refer it to the health competent authority to have designated institutions or professionals form a professional team, assess, and complete an assessment report[7]. The starting point is you, or your agent, applying to the competent authority for the district where your household is registered (戶籍地).

Two ten-day limits and one further assessment follow, all in the text: the assessment report is to reach the health competent authority for the applicant's district of household registration within ten days of completion at the latest, and that authority to forward it to the municipal, county or city competent authority within ten days at the latest; the competent authority must then convene a professional team for a needs assessment, and a disability certificate (身心障礙證明) is issued only where that assessment meets the requirements (it may be run together with the disability assessment)[7]. Article 5 of the same Act defines a person with a disability as someone with impairment or incompleteness in one of eight categories of body-system structure or function, affecting activity and participation in social life — what it recognises is functional impairment, not a disease name. The dimensions and criteria of the assessment sit in a separate regulation whose full text I did not obtain this time; ask the public health or social affairs bureau for your district of household registration.

Labour Insurance, occupational injury and family care leave: the conditions have to hold at the same time

The three requirements in Article 33 of the Labour Insurance Act (勞工保險條例) (amended 114-12-30, promulgated 115-01-21) cannot be met in part: hospitalised for treatment, unable to work, not receiving the original salary, and under treatment; ordinary injury and sickness benefit is paid from the fourth day of inability to work[8]. Radiotherapy or chemotherapy as an outpatient, without admission, does not satisfy that article. The occupational side has a different threshold: Article 42 of the Labour Occupational Accident Insurance and Protection Act (勞工職業災害保險及保護法) has no admission requirement, and benefit may be claimed from the fourth day counting from the day of inability to work, for up to two years[9]. One limitation period as well: the right to claim benefit is extinguished if not exercised within five years of the day it becomes claimable[8].

Where a family member needs leave to provide care, Article 20 of the Act of Gender Equality in Employment (性別平等工作法) (amended 112-07-31, promulgated 112-08-16) reads: family care leave (家庭照顧假) is counted within personal leave, subject to a limit of seven days a year, with pay calculated under the rules for personal leave[10]. It is not extra paid leave on top. The long-term care route need not wait for discharge: you can ask the hospital's discharge-planning team that links patients to long-term care (出院準備銜接長照服務小組) to assess, or ring 1966. The current programme is the Ten-Year Long-Term Care Plan 3.0 (長期照顧十年計畫 3.0) (approved by the Executive Yuan on 31 December 2025, in force from 2026); admission requires an assessed level of long-term care need and membership of one of the statutory age or status categories, and a working-age cancer patient who is simply very short of strength mostly will not qualify. I did not obtain the itemised official document on the admission criteria or on how each county and city handles them, so that is a confirm-on-the-spot item. Details and costs are in "The cost that isn't on the receipt". Verified 30 August 2026.

The cancer registry: the one you do not have to file

Article 11 of the Cancer Control Act (癌症防治法) imposes on cancer control medical institutions the duty of reporting newly occurring cancer cases and data such as stage; Article 12 requires the institution receiving the reports to designate personnel for security maintenance; Article 17 provides a fine where reporting is late[11]. Article 5 of the Cancer Care Quality Assurance Measures Regulations (癌症診療品質保證措施準則) sets the timetable: "an institution shall complete the data registration within one year of the case being confirmed at that institution"[12]. This is the institution's business. You go through no procedure at all.

Two things you can do today

First, dig out your medical certificate of diagnosis, check the date it was issued, work out which day the thirty days runs to, and check that the diagnosis field carries an ICD code. Second, telephone your case manager: has the Catastrophic Illness Certificate application gone in, and on what date. Those two questions take five minutes, and decide how much co-payment you pay over the next several clinic visits. The order of this month as a whole is in "The first month: the boxes that actually have to be ticked"; these statuses are handled differently by each hospital and each county, so ask the NHIA regional division or your household registration district office.

From the physician

These rules go through new editions quickly, so each one here carries its version date; the NHIA's decision and your district authority still govern. Dig out the medical certificate, work out where the thirty days ends, check the diagnosis field carries an ICD code — five minutes. What you paid before approval still has a six-month refund route.
