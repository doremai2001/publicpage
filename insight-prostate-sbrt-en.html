PROSTATE CANCER

Five fractions — with the pelvis included?

101 high-risk patients followed for a median of nearly four years: five-fraction SBRT to prostate and pelvic nodes produced no grade 3 or higher late toxicity.

Insights · Urologic & GI · 5 references
#Prostate#SBRT / SRS#Short Course#Side Effects#NHI Coverage

Five-fraction treatment already has randomised support in low and intermediate risk disease. High risk is different — microscopic nodal involvement is common, so the pelvic nodes are often included in the field, and a larger field means more bladder, rectum and small bowel in the beam. Whether compressing that into five sessions carries a price has been unanswered.

Background: how five fractions became an option

Prostate cancer is no longer a minor entry on Taiwan's cancer map. The Ministry of Health and Welfare's registry analysis for 2022 counted 9,062 new cases in men, ranking it third among male cancers [1]. What has changed the treatment experience most is course length. Radical radiotherapy used to mean 39 hospital visits spread over nearly eight weeks. The PACE-B trial, published in the New England Journal of Medicine in 2024, randomised 874 patients to five-fraction stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT) or a conventional long course and found comparable control at five years [2]. PACE-B, though, enrolled low- and intermediate-risk men with Gleason 3+4 or less, used no androgen deprivation, and irradiated the prostate alone. High-risk disease is a different problem: microscopic nodal spread is common, so the pelvic lymph nodes are usually included in the treated volume. A larger volume pulls more bladder, rectum and small bowel into the beam, and whether that can be compressed into five sessions without a long-term price has been an open question.

What's new: adding the pelvis, five years on

A group at McGill University in Canada filled part of that gap in Practical Radiation Oncology in 2026 [3]. They reviewed 101 men with high-risk prostate cancer treated between August 2019 and May 2023: 36.25 Gy in 5 fractions to the prostate, with a simultaneous 25 Gy in the same 5 fractions to the pelvic lymph nodes, plus 6 to 18 months of androgen deprivation therapy. Everyone had at least two years of follow-up, and the median was 43.8 months. Late gastrointestinal toxicity appeared in 4 patients (4%) — three rectal bleeds and one case of faecal incontinence. Late genitourinary toxicity appeared in 13 (13%), of whom 5 (5%) reached grade 2. Not one grade 3 or higher event occurred. Five-year biochemical recurrence-free survival was 89.5%, distant metastasis-free survival 96.2%, overall survival 98.8%. The authors keep their conclusion modest: the approach "appears feasible". The limits are on the table too — single centre, retrospective, no comparison arm, and every patient received hormone therapy, so the results cannot be credited to radiotherapy alone.

What it means for patients — and for Taiwan

For a patient, this is a safety signal, not a reason to switch course. The verdict rests with randomised data: the UK-led PACE-NODES trial is assigning high-risk men to prostate-only SBRT or prostate plus pelvic nodal SBRT, both delivered in five alternate-day fractions [4]. In Taiwan, SBRT entered National Health Insurance coverage in February 2015, initially only for lung and hepatobiliary tumours. From 1 April 2026 the covered indications widened to include prostate cancer and oligometastatic liver and lung tumours, with roughly 1,500 patients a year expected to benefit [5]. Wider coverage does not make five-fraction pelvic nodal irradiation a routine option. How large the treated volume should be, and whether it can be condensed into five sessions, depends on stage, PSA, imaging and organ dose constraints, and is a case-by-case judgement for the radiation oncologist. One practical point worth settling before treatment starts: NHI pays for the treatment itself, while image-guidance methods, particular platforms (MR-guided or proton systems, for example) and some consumables may carry an out-of-pocket difference — ask your hospital for an itemised estimate. If you are already on treatment, do not request a shorter course or stop hormone therapy on your own because of a headline about five sessions.
