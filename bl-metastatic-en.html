The first line after it has spread

The guidelines and the marketing authorisation have changed which combination goes first; the enfortumab vedotin the NHI pays for is still third line, as a single agent, six courses in a lifetime. Trial figures and clauses word for word, all of it fixed at 13 September 2026.

The number most worth remembering here is not 33.8 months. It is two others: for the 29.1% who had a complete response, the median overall survival of the whole group is still 33.8 months; and by the last assessment, 87% of peripheral neuropathy had not resolved completely.

After it has spread, the international guidelines have changed which combination goes first, and Taiwan's marketing authorisation has changed with them. The reimbursement box has not changed. The date I checked is 13 September 2026: what clause 9.109 of the NHI drug reimbursement regulations pays for (in force since 113/5/1) is enfortumab vedotin, and the clause reads, as a translation of it, "for adult patients with locally advanced or metastatic urothelial carcinoma who have previously received platinum-containing chemotherapy and whose disease has progressed after failure of subsequent treatment with a checkpoint inhibitor (for example an atezolizumab, nivolumab, pembrolizumab, avelumab or ipilimumab preparation)", and also "each patient is reimbursed for a maximum of 6 courses"[1] — third line, as a single agent, six courses in a lifetime. Enfortumab vedotin combined with pembrolizumab in the first line returns zero hits across the whole reimbursement document[1]. Checking the Taiwan FDA's licence file the same day: Padcev (衛部菌疫輸字第001212號, indication updated 2026-05-08) now carries the entry "in combination with pembrolizumab, for the treatment of adult patients with locally advanced or metastatic urothelial carcinoma (mUC)"[2]. The licence is there; the reimbursement is not. I have not titled this article "the first line has changed", and that gap is why.

This box moves fast, and this is the shortest-lived article in the topic. Every figure below carries its trial name, line of therapy, population, number of patients, endpoint and follow-up time, because change the group of people and you change the shape of the answer; and one rule holds from beginning to end: a response rate is not a cure rate. The use around surgery is not here — it is written up on this site in "A thirty-year-old pathway just changed", and I am not restating its figures. I will add only one thing I found on this check: Taiwan's licence for that perioperative use is limited to muscle-invasive bladder cancer in people "not eligible for cisplatin-containing chemotherapy"[2], which is narrower than the American indication that article describes.

What the first-line combination's numbers actually look like

EV-302/KEYNOTE-A39 is a global open-label randomised phase 3 trial in 886 people (enfortumab vedotin plus pembrolizumab 442, chemotherapy 444), with the chemotherapy arm given gemcitabine plus either cisplatin or carboplatin according to whether cisplatin could be used. The analysis I am quoting is the 2.5-year follow-up update: median overall survival 33.8 months (95% confidence interval 26.1 to 39.3; a confidence interval means that if you ran the same study many times over, the answer would land in that range) against 15.9 months (13.6 to 18.3), hazard ratio 0.51 (0.43 to 0.61); median progression-free survival 12.5 against 6.3 months, hazard ratio 0.48 (0.41 to 0.57)[3]. A hazard ratio divides the rate at which events happen in one group by the rate in the other; 0.51 means deaths occurred at roughly half the rate of the control arm, not that people lived twice as long.

The registration analysis (median follow-up 17.2 months) reported 31.5 against 16.1 months, hazard ratio 0.47 (0.38 to 0.58)[4]. Both sets are correct, but they may not be mixed, and whoever quotes one has to say which analysis it came from. With longer follow-up the medians went up and the hazard ratio went up too (0.47 to 0.51) — that is what long follow-up normally does.

A 67.7% response rate, 29.1% of them complete, and then what

Confirmed objective response rate was 67.7% (63.1 to 72.1) against 44.4% (39.7 to 49.2), with the denominator being those who had measurable disease at baseline (437 against 441); the complete response rate was 29.1% against 12.5%[5]. A complete response is not a cure — it means the tumour can no longer be measured on imaging, while in the same trial the median overall survival of the whole group is still 33.8 months[3]. Another number from the same label has to be read alongside it: within the pooled combination-therapy population of 564, of the 373 people with a recorded outcome, only 13% of peripheral neuropathy resolved completely, and 87% still had something left at the last assessment[5].

The longest follow-up available at present is the phase Ib/II cohort of EV-103: 45 people, cisplatin-ineligible, first line, median follow-up 62.1 months, five-year survival 41.5% (26.5 to 56.0)[6]. You can say that some people have lived past five years. You cannot take 41.5% as a general figure for this combination — that is single-arm data from 45 people, and EV-302's follow-up only runs to 2.5 years, so there is no five-year number yet.

The price is not a footnote, it is the main text

Section 6.1 of the FDA label lists EV-302's adverse reactions item by item (safety population 440 against 433): rash 68% and grade 3 to 4 15% (chemotherapy arm 15% and 0), peripheral neuropathy 67% and 8% (14% and 0), diarrhoea 38% and 4.5%, dry eye 24%, and a laboratory rise in blood glucose 66% and 14%. Permanent discontinuation for an adverse reaction 35%, interruption at some point 73%, dose reduction 42%[5]. Section 5 of the label reports on its own pooled populations, not on those 440: the median time to the first grade 2 or worse neuropathy was 6 months, with a range from 0.3 months to 25 months, and that is the 564-patient combination-therapy population[5]. This is not "safer than chemotherapy"; the toxicity is a different shape.

Three things mean contacting the treating team that day, not mentioning it at the next appointment. Stevens-Johnson syndrome and toxic epidermal necrolysis are a boxed warning attached to the drug as a whole, not tied to any one population, and they mostly occur during the first cycle[5] — so, a new rash, particularly with fever or with the lining of the mouth or eyes breaking down. Hyperglycaemia and diabetic ketoacidosis have occurred in people without diabetes too, at a median of 0.5 months in the 720-patient single-agent population[5] — thirst, going to the toilet constantly, glucose-meter readings climbing. The third is new or worsening numbness or weakness in the hands and feet, or getting breathless walking, or a dry cough. None of these waits.

Which group of people these figures were measured on

EV-302 excluded three kinds of patient: active central nervous system metastases, pre-existing grade 2 or worse sensory or motor neuropathy, and poorly controlled diabetes (glycated haemoglobin 8% or above, or 7% or above with symptoms)[7]. All three are common enough in clinic, and they are not in this dataset. The EAU guideline keeps note of one more thing: only three in ten of the control arm went on to switch maintenance with avelumab[8] — the complete version of the chemotherapy road today is chemotherapy followed by maintenance treatment, the control arm did not all get that far, and the gap will be somewhat larger than it would be in practice.

The closest thing to us is the pan-Asian post hoc analysis: 176 people, of whom 51 were in Taiwan; confirmed response rate 72.2% against 35.0%, overall survival hazard ratio 0.33 (0.20 to 0.54)[9]. The sentence the authors added themselves should be copied across: grade 3 or worse skin reactions (28.7%) and hyperglycaemia (10.6%) were numerically higher in the Asian population than in the global one[9]. Post hoc, small numbers: you can read the direction, do not take the multiples away and multiply with them.

For those who cannot have that combination, two roads still carry randomised evidence

The first is platinum-containing chemotherapy followed by avelumab maintenance. JAVELIN Bladder 100 enrolled 700 people who had "had four to six cycles of first-line gemcitabine plus cisplatin or carboplatin and whose disease had not progressed" (no median follow-up is reported): one-year survival 71.3% against 58.4%, median overall survival 21.4 against 14.3 months, hazard ratio 0.69 (0.56 to 0.86); grade 3 or worse adverse events 47.4% against 25.2%[10]. Who it does not apply to belongs in the same paragraph: people whose disease progressed during chemotherapy, people who did not complete four to six cycles, and people who had enfortumab vedotin plus pembrolizumab in the first line. None of them can have this dataset laid over them.

The second is nivolumab added for people fit for cisplatin. That arm of CheckMate 901 had 608 people and a median follow-up of 33.6 months: median overall survival 21.7 against 18.9 months, hazard ratio 0.78 (0.63 to 0.96), P=0.02; complete response 21.7% against 11.8%, with the median duration of complete response 37.1 against 13.2 months; grade 3 or worse adverse events 61.8% against 51.7%[11]. EAU points out two things: this trial enrolled only people fit for cisplatin, and only 9% of its control arm received avelumab maintenance[8] — that second point makes the comparison against "chemotherapy then maintenance" an unequal one.

"Not fit for chemotherapy": three sets of rules, three different answers

The Galsky criteria used in the clinic are five, and meeting any one of them counts as not fit for cisplatin: ECOG performance status of 2 or worse, creatinine clearance below 60 mL/min, grade 2 or worse hearing loss, grade 2 or worse peripheral neuropathy, and New York Heart Association class III heart failure[12]. EAU words it slightly differently: performance status greater than 1, glomerular filtration rate 60 mL/min or below, the other three the same[8]. Taiwan's NHI clause 9.69 is a third set, and word for word it is "adult patients with metastatic urothelial carcinoma who are not suitable for chemotherapy, and who must meet one of the following: i.CTCAE v4.0 grade≧2 audiometric hearing loss ii.CTCAE v4.0 grade≧2 peripheral neuropathy iii.CIRS(the cumulative illness rating scale) score >6", with kidney function set out separately as "pembrolizumab used as a single agent must meet eGFR>30mL/min/1.73m2 and <60mL/min/1.73m2"[1].

The NHI set has no performance status criterion and no heart failure criterion, and uses a cumulative illness rating above 6 instead; kidney function is a band blocked at both ends — too good and you are also outside it. The same person can land in different boxes under the three sets, which is not a matter of one being right and another wrong; it is three things built for different purposes being asked to answer the same question. Below that there is one more box: not fit even for carboplatin. EAU's version is performance status greater than 2, glomerular filtration rate below 30, or performance status 2 together with a filtration rate below 60[8]. What can be quoted for this box is KEYNOTE-052: single-arm, 370 people, median age 74, 19% with an upper tract primary, response rate 29% (24 to 34) and complete response 10%[13]. Single-arm means there was no control group, so that 29% cannot be set beside the hazard ratios of the randomised trials above, and it is not 29% of people cured.

One comparison that holds the figures above upright: before this combination existed, the randomised evidence available to people unfit for cisplatin was EORTC 30986 — 238 people, median follow-up 4.5 years, median overall survival with gemcitabine plus carboplatin 9.3 months[14].

What the NHI pays for is the third line, and it has a lifetime ceiling

The trial that clause 9.109 corresponds to is EV-301: 608 people, the population being those who had had platinum "and" progressed during or after a PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor, median follow-up 11.1 months, median overall survival 12.88 against 8.97 months, hazard ratio 0.70 (0.56 to 0.89)[15]. The clause's phrase "each patient is reimbursed for a maximum of 6 courses" is a lifetime ceiling, not advice on how many cycles to give[1]. The other boxes I am copying across: the nivolumab clause reads "limited to nivolumab in combination with cisplatin and gemcitabine for up to 6 courses, followed by nivolumab as a single agent, as first-line treatment for adult patients with unresectable or metastatic urothelial carcinoma", and requires a glomerular filtration rate of 60 or above; the avelumab maintenance clause reads "after 4 to 6 courses, whose disease has not progressed and who have achieved a partial response (PR) or stable disease (SD)"[1]. That last sentence is not the same as the trial: JAVELIN Bladder 100 enrolled people whose disease "had not progressed", which includes a complete response[10]. I am setting both out as written and not explaining the difference away. PD-L1 is still the gate on payment in Taiwan, and those four rows of the clause are written up in "Which cell actually has a drug in it".

And what I could not find: a reimbursement clause for first-line enfortumab vedotin combined with pembrolizumab, zero hits across the full text[1]. Zero hits means I could not find a listed item. It does not mean "definitely not reimbursed", and it does not mean "so it has to be self-paid" — ask the medical affairs office; how an application would be made is a question for them. I am not writing any amount, because I could not find an official announcement to cite.

On the day of the appointment, get these sentences out loud

  • Which line of therapy is the treatment I am on now? Which trial does it correspond to, and which group of people?

  • My kidney function, hearing, nerves and performance status — which box do they land in under each of Galsky's five, EAU's version, and the three items in NHI clause 9.69?

  • If I go with enfortumab vedotin plus pembrolizumab: what is my glycated haemoglobin, and did I have neuropathy before this started? (The trial excluded people who were poor on those two.)

  • If I go with chemotherapy: after four to six cycles, do I go on to maintenance treatment, and do I meet the PR or SD the clause requires?

  • For the boxes where I could not find a listed item in the NHI — what does the application process look like? Please take that one straight to the medical affairs office or my case manager.

Once more on how long this page is good for. Every licence and every reimbursement clause above stops at 13 September 2026, and the trial figures only run to what was published by that day. When you read this, ask what the position is now rather than using it as today's rule; and do not stop or change a course of treatment on the strength of it.

From the physician

Which line of therapy you are on decides which set of figures applies to you, so settle that question first. If you are going with enfortumab vedotin plus pembrolizumab, look up two things before the appointment: your glycated haemoglobin, and whether your hands and feet were already numb — the trial excluded people who were poor on both. For the boxes where no listed item can be found in the NHI, ask your case manager to take you to the medical affairs office for the application process, rather than looking up a price online.
